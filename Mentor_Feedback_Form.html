About the Learning Curve (Mentor Assessment):

Inspiration is taken from the word “learning curve”, which literally means the trajectory of learning and growth over time. Mentors are assessing where mentees are in their journey of becoming HC facilitators and where they need to be.

It is not a judging or rating parameter, but a specific measure of your progress standpoint. It gives mentors clarity about the parameters used to assess mentees and supports constructive reflection on their growth.

The mentee, recording date, module, section, and recording link are fetched from the selected submission. Mentor identity comes from your facilitator login.

4. "Responsiveness to chat" (Presentation)

  • Reacting to and calling out chat responses.
  • Taking them individually if required and fostering participation in the process.
  • Ability to quickly grasp any key themes that emerge in the chat responses and reflect this to participants (useful when multiple responses pour in and it is not practical or connecting to read them all out).
Curve Point Description (Responsiveness to chat)
3
  • the mentee respond promptly and consistently to messages or queries.
  • Responses are clear, thoughtful, and show active engagement with the discussion.
  • Paraphrased the participant's question OR tried to empathise with the participants sensing into their feelings and needs when the situation was very alive in the participant.
  • the mentee ask follow-up questions and provide relevant input (whenever required).
2
  • the mentee responds within a reasonable timeframe, but there may be occasional delays or incomplete responses.
  • the mentee's contributions are generally relevant, though sometimes they lack depth.
1
  • the mentee may be slow to respond or rarely engage in the conversation.
  • the mentee's responses may be brief or incomplete.

5. "Concept Clarity" (Presentation)

  • Clarity in sharing of concepts, with simple, interactive delivery supported with relevant examples, preferably from own experience, so that it doesn't remain academic only.
  • Confidently answering questions of participants and making learning interesting through insightful answers.
  • Concept clarity can also be ascertained through your slides (pptx doc) if you make any.
Curve Point Description (Concept Clarity)
5
  • the mentee demonstrates exceptional clarity in explaining concepts.
  • The delivery is clear, simple, and interactive, with a focus on making NVC principles easy to understand.
  • the mentee consistently use relevant, relatable examples (including personal experiences) to connect theory to real-world situations.
  • the mentee answers participants’ questions confidently and insightfully, providing thoughtful and detailed responses that deepen understanding.
  • the mentee is confident and flexible reflecting back the essence of what the mentee understands a question or contribution, and can use the questions to support the questioner’s own learning, or open them out to others.
  • the mentee's slides (if used) are well-designed, visually engaging, and enhance the clarity of the concept.
  • The overall presentation fosters interest, and participants leave with a solid understanding of the topic.
4
  • the mentee explains concepts with good clarity and simplicity.
  • the mentee offer clear explanations and use examples.
  • the mentee's delivery is interactive, though there could be slightly more participant involvement at times.
  • the mentee answers most questions confidently, with insightful responses that generally enhance understanding.
  • the mentee is able to reflect back the mentee's understanding of a question or contribution, and may sometimes use questions to support the questioner’s own learning, or open them out to others.
3
  • the mentee provides adequate accuracy in explaining concepts, but the mentee's explanations may sometimes lack clarity or may not be as engaging.
  • the mentee try to use examples, but these may not always feel relevant to the topic or feel vague.
  • While the mentee answers questions, some answers may lack depth or might not fully clarify the concept for participants.
  • the mentee is not at ease reflecting back accurately the mentee's understanding of a question or contribution, or of using questions to support the questioner’s own learning, or opening them out to others.
2
  • the mentee's explanations of concepts are sometimes unclear or overly complex.
  • the mentee may have struggled to break down ideas in simple terms, and the delivery can feel one-sided or lack interaction.
  • the mentee offer examples, but these may be vague or not clearly connected to the topic at hand.
  • Answers to questions may be unclear or incomplete, leaving some confusion among participants.
  • the mentee does not reflect back understanding of a question or engage the questioner.
  • Overall, the presentation may not fully engage participants or may require follow-up to clarify key concepts.
1
  • the mentee struggles with clarity in explaining concepts.
  • the mentor observes the mentee's explanations are either overly complex or too vague, making it difficult for participants to grasp the material.
  • the mentee rarely use examples, or the examples provided are irrelevant, leaving the learning feeling academic or disconnected from real-world application.
  • Responses to questions are often inaccurate, unclear, or lack sufficient detail, leading to confusion.
  • the mentee does not reflect back understanding of a question or engage the questioner.
  • The delivery lacks engagement, and participants may leave with a limited understanding of the topic.

6. "Walking the Talk" (Presentation)

Quality of presence of presenter: Greeting, smiling, establishing warm/approachable presence through facial expressions.

Connection of the presenter with Self and the participants. To what extent is the presenter listening and speaking in accord with LPoC, HC consciousness, principles and practices.

Note: A strong connection with self means being aligned with inner values and principles and bringing your true, authentic self to interactions—including acknowledging vulnerability with composure (e.g., "the mentee is not sure of the answer right now, will get back").

Example of gap: Interrupting, usage of "should" / "must", alienating language, not empathizing, providing quick-fix solutions.

Curve Point Description (Walking the talk)
5
  • the mentee consistently demonstrates an outstanding quality of presence (e.g., connecting the dots on what has been shared in chat or plenary for conceptual clarity, smiling, not reading directly from notes).
  • the mentee greets others warmly, smile often, and maintain a highly approachable and open demeanor throughout interactions.
  • the mentee makes a strong connection with the mentor and the participants.
  • the mentee listen deeply and engage fully, embodying the principles of LPoC, HC, and relevant practices.
  • the mentee's speaking and actions reflect a high level of consciousness and alignment with the mentee's stated values (e.g., responding in a composed manner using LPoC in triggering situations, connecting rather than correcting, inviting participants to contemplate).
4
  • the mentee maintains a warm and approachable presence, offering consistent greetings and displaying openness through non-verbal communication (facial expressions) or encouraging words.
  • the mentee engage in active listening, and while there may be occasional lapses, the mentee's presence generally aligns with the principles of LPoC and HC.
  • the mentee's words and actions are mostly in accord with the stated principles, and the mentee makes a good connection with the mentor and the participants, even if some opportunities for deeper connection are occasionally missed.
3
  • the mentee demonstrates an adequate presence but may have moments of disengagement or inconsistency in warmth (e.g., sometimes greeting or smiling less).
  • the mentee listens and responds to others, but the mentee's connection to participants or the mentor may feel more surface-level.
  • the mentee's adherence to the principles of LPoC, HC, and other practices is apparent but not always consistent.
2
  • the mentee occasionally speaks in a way that contradicts the principles of LPoC, HC, or other related practices.
  • While there are moments of connection, these are not frequent, and the mentee's presence may feel disconnected or misaligned with the mentee's values at times.
1
  • It is challenging to connect with the participants.
  • the mentee struggle with active listening and often speak in ways that contradict the principles of LPoC and HC.
  • the mentee's connection to self and participants is limited, and there is a noticeable lack of alignment between the mentee's words, actions, and values.
