About This Self-Evaluation (Learning Curve Standpoint):

Inspiration is taken from the word “learning curve”, which literally means the trajectory of learning and growth over time. We are trying to ascertain where you are in your journey of becoming an HC facilitator and where you need to be.

It is not a judging or rating parameter, but a specific measure of your progress standpoint. It gives a clear understanding of the expectations of the mentoring process and helps both you and your mentors reflect constructively on your growth.

6. "Responsiveness to chat" (Presentation)

  • Reacting to and calling out chat responses.
  • Taking them individually if required and fostering participation in the process.
  • Ability to quickly grasp any key themes that emerge in the chat responses and reflect this to participants (useful when multiple responses pour in and it is not practical or connecting to read them all out).
Curve Point Description (Responsiveness to chat)
3
  • I respond promptly and consistently to messages or queries.
  • Responses are clear, thoughtful, and show active engagement with the discussion.
  • Paraphrased the participant's question OR tried to empathise with the participants sensing into their feelings and needs when the situation was very alive in the participant.
  • I ask follow-up questions and provide relevant input (whenever required).

  • My contributions are generally relevant, though sometimes they lack depth.

7. "Concept Clarity" (Presentation)

  • Clarity in sharing of concepts, with simple, interactive delivery supported with relevant examples, preferably from own experience, so that it doesn't remain academic only.
  • Confidently answering questions of participants and making learning interesting through insightful answers.
  • Concept clarity can also be ascertained through your slides (pptx doc) if you make any.
Curve Point Description (Concept Clarity)
5
  • I demonstrate exceptional clarity in explaining concepts.
  • The delivery is clear, simple, and interactive, with a focus on making NVC principles easy to understand.
  • I consistently use relevant, relatable examples (including personal experiences) to connect theory to real-world situations.
  • I answer participants’ questions confidently and insightfully, providing thoughtful and detailed responses that deepen understanding.
  • I am confident and flexible reflecting back the essence of what I understand of a question or contribution, and can use the questions to support the questioner’s own learning, or open them out to others.
  • My slides (if used) are well-designed, visually engaging, and enhance the clarity of the concept.
  • The overall presentation fosters interest, and participants leave with a solid understanding of the topic.
4
  • I explain concepts with good clarity and simplicity.
  • I offer clear explanations and use examples.
  • My delivery is interactive, though there could be slightly more participant involvement at times.
  • I answer most questions confidently, with insightful responses that generally enhance understanding.
  • I am able to reflect back my understanding of a question or contribution, and may sometimes use questions to support the questioner’s own learning, or open them out to others.
3
  • I provide adequate accuracy in explaining concepts, but my explanations may sometimes lack clarity or may not be as engaging.
  • I try to use examples, but these may not always feel relevant to the topic or feel vague.
  • While I answer questions, some answers may lack depth or might not fully clarify the concept for participants.
  • I am not at ease reflecting back accurately my understanding of a question or contribution, or of using questions to support the questioner’s own learning, or opening them out to others.
2
  • My explanations of concepts are sometimes unclear or overly complex.
  • I may have struggled to break down ideas in simple terms, and the delivery can feel one-sided or lack interaction.
  • I offer examples, but these may be vague or not clearly connected to the topic at hand.
  • Answers to questions may be unclear or incomplete, leaving some confusion among participants.
  • I do not reflect back understanding of a question or engage the questioner.
  • Overall, the presentation may not fully engage participants or may require follow-up to clarify key concepts.
1
  • I struggle with clarity in explaining concepts.
  • I feel my explanations are either overly complex or too vague, making it difficult for participants to grasp the material.
  • I rarely use examples, or the examples provided are irrelevant, leaving the learning feeling academic or disconnected from real-world application.
  • Responses to questions are often inaccurate, unclear, or lack sufficient detail, leading to confusion.
  • I do not reflect back understanding of a question or engage the questioner.
  • The delivery lacks engagement, and participants may leave with a limited understanding of the topic.

8. "Walking the Talk" (Presentation)

Quality of presence of presenter: Greeting, smiling, establishing warm/approachable presence through facial expressions.

Connection of the presenter with Self and the participants. To what extent is the presenter listening and speaking in accord with LPoC, HC consciousness, principles and practices.

Note: A strong connection with self means being aligned with inner values and principles and bringing your true, authentic self to interactions—including acknowledging vulnerability with composure (e.g., "I am not sure of the answer right now, will get back").

Example of gap: Interrupting, usage of "should" / "must", alienating language, not empathizing, providing quick-fix solutions.

Curve Point Description (Walking the talk)
5
  • I consistently demonstrate an outstanding quality of presence (e.g., connecting the dots on what has been shared in chat or plenary for conceptual clarity, smiling, not reading directly from notes).
  • I greet others warmly, smile often, and maintain a highly approachable and open demeanor throughout interactions.
  • I make a strong connection with both myself and the participants.
  • I listen deeply and engage fully, embodying the principles of LPoC, HC, and relevant practices.
  • My speaking and actions reflect a high level of consciousness and alignment with my stated values (e.g., responding in a composed manner using LPoC in triggering situations, connecting rather than correcting, inviting participants to contemplate).
4
  • I maintain a warm and approachable presence, offering consistent greetings and displaying openness through non-verbal communication (facial expressions) or encouraging words.
  • I engage in active listening, and while there may be occasional lapses, my presence generally aligns with the principles of LPoC and HC.
  • My words and actions are mostly in accord with the stated principles, and I make a good connection with both myself and the participants, even if some opportunities for deeper connection are occasionally missed.
3
  • I demonstrate an adequate presence but may have moments of disengagement or inconsistency in warmth (e.g., sometimes greeting or smiling less).
  • I listen and respond to others, but my connection to participants or myself may feel more surface-level.
  • My adherence to the principles of LPoC, HC, and other practices is apparent but not always consistent.
2
  • I occasionally speak in a way that contradicts the principles of LPoC, HC, or other related practices.
  • While there are moments of connection, these are not frequent, and my presence may feel disconnected or misaligned with my values at times.
1
  • It is challenging to connect with the participants.
  • I struggle with active listening and often speak in ways that contradict the principles of LPoC and HC.
  • My connection to self and participants is limited, and there is a noticeable lack of alignment between my words, actions, and values.
